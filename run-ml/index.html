🧠 Run ML

Upload your runs. Three machine learning models compete to predict your 5K, and the winner plans your next 4 weeks.

1. Your runs

An export from your running app or watch works as is. Or a CSV with date, distance_km, duration_min, type.

🔒Everything runs in your browser. Your file is never uploaded anywhere.

How do I get my runs?

From your running app or watch: most of them let you download your activities as a CSV file, usually under account or privacy settings ("export data" or "download your data"). Upload that file as is; non-running activities are skipped.

type is optional: easy, long, tempo, intervals or race. If you leave it out, it's guessed from your pace. You need at least 12 races or tempo runs and about 2 months of history.

2. The models competed

Each model was trained on your older runs and tested on newer ones it had never seen: always train on the past, test on the future. Bars show the average error in seconds. Shorter is better.

3. What matters most for your 5K

How much worse the winning model gets (in seconds) when it can't use each piece of information. Bigger means more important for you.

4. Your best 4-week plans

The winning model tried about 70 plans and predicted your 5K at the end of each. Mileage never grows more than 10% a week.

The model only knows what your own training has shown it. Treat this as a guide, not a promise, and back off if anything hurts.

How it works

The short version

1. Every hard effort becomes a training example. Each race or tempo run is turned into the 5K time it's "worth" (Riegel's formula: T₅ₖ = T × (5 / km)^1.06).

2. Features describe the training before it: fitness (6-week load), fatigue (1-week load), freshness, km per week, runs per week, hard sessions, long run, rest days and recent best.

3. Three models compete with time-series cross-validation, against a simple baseline ("you'll run your recent best"). The one with the smallest error on future runs wins.

4. The winner plans: each candidate plan becomes a pretend run log for the next 4 weeks, and the model predicts your 5K at the end.

It's the same Python code as the command-line tool (pandas and scikit-learn), running in your browser with Pyodide.
